## A calendar is a social surface

People treat calendars as private tools, but on small teams they quickly become a shared agreement about time. Sloppy events, unclear titles, and placeholder holds do more than create clutter. They make the whole system harder to trust.

## The habits that help most

We recommend a short hygiene baseline:

- name the meeting after the decision or outcome it supports
- remove holds that no longer matter
- leave visible room for focus instead of scheduling every gap

These habits sound administrative, but they change the emotional texture of a week. The calendar starts to feel like a map instead of a pileup.

## Why it belongs in product design

Calendar hygiene is not just a user habit. It is influenced by how software presents default titles, reminders, states, and follow-up prompts. Good products gently reinforce good calendar behavior. Bad ones normalize noise.

We want Orgo to push in the first direction.
