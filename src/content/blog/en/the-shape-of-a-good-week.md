## A good week has structure, not total control

When people say they want a productive week, they often picture a perfectly filled calendar. In practice, the best weeks usually have more slack than that. They have enough structure to protect what matters and enough breathing room to absorb what reality adds.

We think of a good week as a matter of proportion.

## The proportions we aim for

Most weeks benefit from a small set of anchors:

- one or two commitments that genuinely matter
- a short list of follow-through work
- a place to capture loose ideas without promoting them too early

Everything else should fit around that shape, not compete with it.

## Why this matters to the product

Planning tools often create pressure by making every captured item look equally urgent. We want Orgo to distinguish between material that needs action, material that needs reflection, and material that simply needs a place to rest until it becomes clearer.

That distinction is subtle, but it changes the tone of the week. Instead of feeling judged by an infinitely expanding list, you get a clearer sense of what deserves your energy right now.
