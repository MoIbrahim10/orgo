## Search is often asked to repair a bad system

When organization fails, search becomes the emergency exit. That is why many search interfaces feel overloaded: they are carrying the burden of missing context, weak naming, and too many disconnected surfaces.

## What we want from search instead

We want search in Orgo to do more than find text matches. It should help people recover the shape of a thought:

- where they saw it
- when it mattered
- what it was connected to

That changes result design. Titles matter, but surrounding metadata matters too. A result should help you recognize something before you even click into it.

## Recognition beats precision in real work

Under pressure, people rarely remember exact phrasing. They remember fragments, moods, and adjacent ideas. Search that supports recognition feels faster because it meets human memory where it actually lives.

That is why we think of search as part of the editorial system, not just a utility bar sitting above it.
