## Editing is interface work

A surprising amount of usability comes from decisions that never look visual in a screenshot. The exact length of a label, the order of words in a button, the difference between a warning and a nudge. These are editing problems, but they are also design problems.

## What we cut first

When a screen feels noisy, we usually look for:

- repeated concepts hiding behind different labels
- helper text that explains instead of guiding
- decorative adjectives that slow scanning

Good editing rarely makes a screen feel emptier. It makes the next action feel easier to trust.

## Why we care so much

Orgo is trying to support people during crowded weeks. In that setting, a slightly clearer sentence is not cosmetic. It changes how quickly someone regains orientation. That is why we treat editing as part of product craft, not a final polish pass.
