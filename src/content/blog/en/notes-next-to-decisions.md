## The cost of separated context

Teams rarely lose track of work because nothing was written down. They lose track because the _reasoning_ behind the work was written somewhere else.

A meeting note explains why a choice was made. A task manager records the next action. A calendar tells you when to return to it. The problem is that those three pieces often end up in three different systems. By the time the task resurfaces, it has become an instruction without memory.

## What we changed inside Orgo

We now treat a decision as a small cluster instead of a single line item:

- the note that captured the problem
- the point where a choice became real
- the action that should follow from it

That structure sounds obvious, but it changes how a week feels. Review becomes less about reconstructing the past and more about moving forward with intact context.

## Why it matters beyond one feature

This is also why the journal exists. Shipping a capability is easy to announce. Leaving behind a useful record is harder. We want the product and the writing to share the same discipline: if something matters, the surrounding context should be easy to recover later.

When memory is built into the workflow, people do not need heroic habits just to stay oriented.
