## Taxonomy should reduce thinking, not create more of it

The fastest way to make a notes system unusable is to ask people to decide too much at capture time. If every thought needs a perfect folder, tag set, and project assignment before it can exist, the system will be avoided.

## The distinction we rely on

Inside Orgo, we think about note organization in three layers:

- **projects** for work that has an outcome
- **places** for where context tends to live
- **tags** for qualities that may cut across both

The order matters. Projects and places are relatively stable. Tags are lighter and should stay optional.

## The rule of maintenance

Every organizing surface creates future maintenance. That is why we bias toward fewer categories with clearer meanings. The right question is not "can this be tagged?" but "will this still help three weeks from now when I am looking for something under pressure?"

If the answer is uncertain, the simplest structure usually wins.
