## Translation is not enough

Microcopy often fails in bilingual products because the team treats it as a tiny translation task. But labels and helper text do more than carry meaning. They set tone, pace decisions, and signal how formal or forgiving the interface feels.

Those qualities do not transfer word-for-word.

## What we optimize for

When we write short interface copy for Orgo, we look for three things:

- the action should be unmistakable
- the tone should match the stress level of the moment
- the line should sound natural in both languages, even if the wording differs

That sometimes means letting Arabic and English diverge slightly. Fidelity to intent matters more than literal symmetry.

## The hidden design effect

Microcopy changes layout. A sharper label can rescue hierarchy. A calmer helper sentence can make a form feel less accusatory. In a bilingual interface, those changes ripple into spacing, scanning order, and perceived complexity.

That is why we write in conversation with design and engineering. The best microcopy is not just correct. It changes how the interface _behaves_.
